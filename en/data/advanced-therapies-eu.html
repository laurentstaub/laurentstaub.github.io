Dataset · Advanced therapies · European Union

Version 1.0 · census as at 2026-07-27 · one row per centralised marketing authorisation

This dataset lists every centralised marketing authorisation granted to an advanced therapy medicinal product in the European Union since 2009.

33 authorisations granted since 2009, 24 in force, 9 ended, 22 marketed. By category: 24 gene therapies, 5 somatic cell therapies, 4 tissue-engineered products.

Counting conventions

A count with no stated convention means nothing. Those used here:

  • Unit: the authorisation. Zolgensma and Itvisma share a substance but count as two; Durveqtix and Beqvez are one renamed authorisation and count as one.
  • Authorised is not marketed: the Status column distinguishes the two, and only 22 products are actually marketed.

External consistency check. The 27 authorisations dated before 30 November 2024 correspond exactly to the count published by Frontiers in Medicine at that same date, withdrawals included.

Derived parameters

Of the nine observed exits: seven for commercial reasons, one for efficacy (Alofisel), one for manufacturing (MACI). Median time between grant and termination: 38 months, from 4 months (Skysona) to 82 months (Chondrocelect).

The crude 27 % exit rate is neither a five-year nor a seven-year risk: it mixes very unequal follow-up times. The Kaplan-Meier estimate gives 18 % at five years and 45 % at seven years (confidence interval 30 to 79 % at 82 months). Median survival is not reached. Numbers at risk: 33 at origin, 24 at 24 months, 18 at 48 months, 10 at 72 months, 4 at 96 months. Cumulative incidence by cause: commercial 18.1 % at 5 years and 31.8 % at 7 years; efficacy 7.8 %; manufacturing 5.8 %.

Across the 33 authorisations: 24 rest on a single-arm pivotal trial, for a median pivotal sample of 64 patients; 14 are conditional and 3 fall under exceptional circumstances.

Method and sources

Every row is established against: the product's European public assessment report, the register of authorisations, the Agency's public withdrawal statements, the decisions published in the Commission's union register and, for the most recent authorisation, the Official Journal of the European Union. The advanced therapy sub-category comes from the Committee's quarterly report, the only source that states it for all products.

Residual uncertainties

Fields that verification could not establish are flagged as such in the census file. They concern mainly Itvisma, whose assessment report is not yet published — authorisation type and pivotal data to be re-checked on publication; the exact pivotal sample of Breyanzi, not publicly established; the formal designation of the Spherox pivotal trial; and the sample size of Zemcelpro, given as 25 in the public summary and 36 in the assessment report.
